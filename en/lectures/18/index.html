Lecture 18

Resolvents as analytic functions

What this lecture does

A bounded operator has a holomorphic inverse away from its spectrum. Contour integrals of that inverse give projections and analytic simple eigenvalue branches. For a bounded Friedrichs model we derive the scalar resolvent, continue it across an interval and prove its leading resonance decay rate.

18.4 A bounded Friedrichs model

With zero coupling these evolve independently; the off-diagonal terms allow exchange between them. The coupling function specifies how strongly each continuum energy participates. 18.6 The compact spectral input

An operator is compact if every bounded sequence has a subsequence whose images converge in norm.

Theorem 18.8 (Compact self-adjoint spectral theorem cited). A compact self-adjoint operator has an orthonormal basis of eigenvectors, including a basis of its kernel. Its nonzero eigenvalues are real, have finite multiplicity, and can accumulate only at zero.

This is an external input, from Axler, Theorem 10.106 with 10.82 and 10.93. None of the resolvent or bounded-model proofs above used it. The general completeness deduction in Lecture 24 uses it and inherits that qualification.

Summary

  • Bounded resolvents are norm holomorphic. With (A−zI)−1(A-zI)^{-1}(A−zI)−1 the Riesz projection carries a minus sign. For a norm-holomorphic bounded family, an isolated rank-one contour projection gives a holomorphic simple eigenvalue branch.
  • A supplied nonnegative integrable density is recovered by Stieltjes inversion of its Cauchy transform.
  • The bounded Friedrichs Schur formula gives a scalar continuation. For a locally analytic positive density its simple lower pole has the proved leading decay rate 2πg2ρ(λ0)/ℏ2\pi g^2\rho(\lambda_0)/\hbar2πg2ρ(λ0​)/ℏ.
  • Compact self-adjoint spectral completeness remains a cited input.
