Lecture 21

Jacobi functions and the pendulum

The pendulum period is an elliptic integral. Its motion is described by the inverse integral, a Jacobi function. We construct the real Jacobi functions by an ordinary differential equation, prove their periods and verify the pendulum formula with its initial conditions. The limit of these librations is a separatrix motion with infinite period.

By the end of this lecture you will be able to:

  • Use the Jacobi invariants to prove existence for all real time.
  • Recover the quarter-period from the complete elliptic integral.
  • Verify the positive-crossing pendulum solution and its least period.
  • Prove compact-time convergence to the separatrix as the modulus tends to one.

Summary

The real Jacobi ODE has a bounded global solution. Its first quarter inverts an elliptic integral, and uniqueness supplies the remaining quarters. The positive-crossing pendulum formula follows by differentiation.

  • The libration amplitude satisfies k=sin⁡(θmax⁡/2)k=\sin(\theta_{\max}/2)k=sin(θmax​/2), and its least period is 4K(k)/ν04K(k)/\nu_04K(k)/ν0​ for 0<k<10<k<10<k<1.
  • As k↑1k\uparrow1k↑1 the motion approaches the separatrix on compact time intervals while its period diverges.
