Lecture 24

Regular Sturm–Liouville problems

After a suitable positive shift, a regular second-order boundary problem has a symmetric Green kernel. Its inverse is compact on the correctly weighted Hilbert space. We prove the kernel construction and elementary eigenvalue properties. General completeness then follows from the cited compact spectral theorem.

By the end of this lecture you will be able to:

  • Retain the endpoint terms in Green’s identity for Robin data.
  • Prove reality, simplicity and weighted orthogonality of eigenfunctions.
  • Construct the Green kernel with its signed flux jump.
  • Deduce completeness with the stated compact-spectral qualification.

24.5 Compactness and qualified completeness

The same kernel defines a bounded operator on H\mathcal HH. Indeed, Cauchy–Schwarz gives ∣Tf(x)∣≤∥G(x,⋅)∥w∥f∥w|Tf(x)|\le\norm{G(x,\cdot)}_w\norm f_w∣Tf(x)∣≤∥G(x,⋅)∥w​∥f∥w​. Continuity of GGG on the compact square makes the first factor uniformly bounded. It also makes TfTfTf continuous, because ∥G(x,⋅)−G(x0,⋅)∥w→0\norm{G(x,\cdot)-G(x_0,\cdot)}_w\to0∥G(x,⋅)−G(x0​,⋅)∥w​→0 as x→x0x\to x_0x→x0​. Symmetry and Fubini give ⟨f,Th⟩w=⟨Tf,h⟩w\inner f{Th}_w=\inner{Tf}h_w⟨f,Th⟩w​=⟨Tf,h⟩w​. Fubini applies since the bounded kernel multiplies two L1(w dx)L^1(w\,\dd x)L1(wdx) functions; finite measure and Cauchy–Schwarz give that integrability.

Uniform continuity approximates GGG uniformly by kernels constant on each rectangle of a finite partition. Their operators have finite-dimensional range, spanned by the interval indicators. A uniform kernel error ε\varepsilonε gives operator-norm error at most ε∫abw\varepsilon\int_a^b wε∫ab​w by the same Cauchy–Schwarz estimate. Hence TTT is compact. To justify the last implication, take a bounded sequence and extract nested subsequences on which the images under each finite-rank approximant converge. On the diagonal subsequence, the operator-norm error makes the TTT-images Cauchy. Completeness supplies their limit, as required by the definition of compactness.

The kernel of TTT is zero. For ϕ∈Cc∞((a,b))\phi\in C_c^\infty((a,b))ϕ∈Cc∞​((a,b)), put h=(L+c0w)ϕ/wh=(L+c_0w)\phi/wh=(L+c0​w)ϕ/w, which is continuous. The classical inverse theorem gives Th=ϕTh=\phiTh=ϕ. If Tf=0Tf=0Tf=0, self-adjointness gives ⟨f,ϕ⟩w=⟨f,Th⟩w=⟨Tf,h⟩w=0\inner f\phi_w=\inner f{Th}_w=\inner{Tf}h_w=0⟨f,ϕ⟩w​=⟨f,Th⟩w​=⟨Tf,h⟩w​=0. Smooth density, Theorem 5.8, and equivalence of the weighted norm force f=0f=0f=0.

24.6 The sine basis recovered

For p=w=1p=w=1p=w=1 and q=0q=0q=0, impose Dirichlet data on [0,ℓ][0,\ell][0,ℓ], with ℓ>0\ell>0ℓ>0. Here ℓ\ellℓ denotes the interval length, since LLL denotes the operator. Summary

  • Real separated Robin conditions cancel the skew boundary form. They give real simple eigenvalues and weighted orthogonality.
  • Endpoint trace bounds permit a positive shift despite possibly negative Robin energy terms.
  • Two homogeneous solutions give a continuous symmetric Green kernel with flux jump −1-1−1. Its weighted integral operator is compact and injective.
  • General completeness inherits the cited compact spectral theorem. The Dirichlet sine case also has the independent proved Fourier argument.
