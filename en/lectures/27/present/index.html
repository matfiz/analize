Lecture 27

The scalar Riemann–Hilbert problem

A prescribed boundary jump determines an analytic function on each side of a contour once a normalisation removes the entire-function ambiguity. We solve an additive problem on the real line and then one rational Wiener–Hopf problem. The upper and lower half-plane conventions determine every factor and every time-support sign.

By the end of this lecture you will be able to:

  • Solve a compact smooth additive jump by a normalised Cauchy integral.
  • Prove uniqueness by gluing and Liouville’s theorem.
  • Factor the chosen rational coefficient and solve its boundary equation.
  • Convert the upper and lower solution components to one-sided time functions.

27.1 Boundary values and gluing

Orient the real axis from left to right. Its left side is the upper half-plane, denoted by +++; the lower half-plane is denoted by −-−. A scalar Riemann–Hilbert problem prescribes a relation between the boundary values of scalar holomorphic functions on the two sides. Here boundary values are continuous extensions from each closed half-plane. The normalisation O(1/z)O(1/z)O(1/z) means a bound C/∣z∣C/|z|C/∣z∣ uniform in that half-plane for sufficiently large ∣z∣|z|∣z∣.

Lemma 27.1 (Gluing). Suppose F+F_+F+​ and F−F_-F−​ are holomorphic on the upper and lower half-planes, continuous up to the real line, and agree there. Their union is entire.

Proof. The union is continuous. Take a triangle crossing the real line, initially with no vertex on that line and no edge contained in it. Cut its upper portion along height ε\varepsilonε and its lower portion along height −ε-\varepsilon−ε. Each resulting polygon is contained in a half-plane, so its boundary integral is zero by triangulation and the Cauchy theorem, Theorem 9.4. As ε↓0\varepsilon\downarrow0ε↓0, their exterior edges tend to the original triangle boundary. The omitted edge segments have length O(ε)O(\varepsilon)O(ε) and bounded integrand, so their integrals tend to zero. The horizontal cut integrals have opposite orientations. Continuity on a compact neighbourhood makes them converge to the same real-line integral, so they cancel. The triangle integral of the union is therefore zero. Triangles in the exceptional positions are limits of these triangles; continuity again passes their integrals to the limit. Morera’s theorem, Theorem 10.8, now proves holomorphy everywhere. 

If both functions also satisfy the stated O(1/z)O(1/z)O(1/z) bound, the glued entire function tends to zero at infinity. It is bounded on the remaining compact disk, so Liouville’s theorem, Theorem 10.4, makes it identically zero. This will be the uniqueness argument for both problems.

Summary

  • For h∈Cc1(R)h\in C_c^1(\R)h∈Cc1​(R), the normalised Cauchy integral has jump hhh and is the unique uniformly decaying additive solution continuous up to the axis.
  • Matching continuous boundary values glue holomorphically. Uniform decay then permits Liouville’s uniqueness argument.
  • The chosen positive rational coefficient has index zero and an explicit product factorisation, giving the stated unique Wiener–Hopf pair.
  • Under the positive-exponent time transform, positive-time support corresponds to the upper solution component. General index-dependent existence is cited.
