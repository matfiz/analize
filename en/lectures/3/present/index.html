Lecture 3

The Lebesgue integral: measure and convergence

Pointwise limits can leave the class of Riemann integrable functions. The Lebesgue integral allows more functions and gives convergence theorems with explicit hypotheses. We take measure and monotone convergence as standing facts. From them we prove Fatou’s lemma, dominated convergence and a criterion for integrating an infinite sum.

By the end of this lecture you will be able to:

  • Identify a failure of completeness in the Riemann integral distance.
  • Check the hypotheses of monotone and dominated convergence.
  • Justify integration of a series by absolute integrability.
  • Distinguish convergence almost everywhere from convergence in integral norm.

3.1 Where the Riemann integral fails

Integrals in this section are Riemann integrals. A uniform limit of Riemann integrable functions on a bounded interval is Riemann integrable, and the integrals converge by Theorem 1.2. Pointwise convergence does not have this property.

3.2 Standing facts for Lebesgue integration

We now use the Lebesgue integral. Its construction is supplied by the standing facts M1–M3. Under the course’s proof policy, these cited facts do not change a subsequent proof’s status to sketched.
