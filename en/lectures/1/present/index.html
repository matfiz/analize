Lecture 1

Uniform limits and fixed points

Physics uses limits inside integrals, derivatives and equations. This lecture establishes uniform bounds that justify these operations. Completeness turns successive approximations into a solution. The contraction principle then gives existence, uniqueness and an error bound.

By the end of this lecture you will be able to:

  • Distinguish pointwise convergence from uniform convergence.
  • Check the hypotheses for integrating or differentiating a limit.
  • Differentiate a power series inside its convergence disk.
  • Use the contraction principle with an explicit error bound.

Summary

  • The space C(K)C(K)C(K) is complete in the supremum norm for nonempty compact KKK.
  • Uniform limits of continuous functions are continuous. Uniform limits of Riemann integrable functions on a bounded interval preserve their integrals. Uniform convergence of the derivatives, with convergence at one point, justifies differentiating the limit.
  • A power series and its derivatives converge uniformly on smaller concentric closed disks inside its radius of convergence. Its sum is complex-differentiable there, with the termwise derivative.
  • A contraction on a nonempty closed subset of a Banach space has a unique fixed point, with the iteration error bounded by Equation (1.2).
