Lecture 22

Theta functions and Poisson summation

Summing a rapidly decreasing function over integer translates produces a periodic function. Its Fourier coefficients prove Poisson summation, the result deferred from Lecture 7. Applied to a Gaussian, it gives the theta modular law. We also prove quasi-periodicity, count the zeros and derive the heat equation in the same normalisation.

By the end of this lecture you will be able to:

  • Prove Poisson summation for Schwartz functions with the course constants.
  • Derive theta quasi-periodicity and identify all its simple zeros.
  • Verify the heat equation by justified termwise differentiation.
  • Derive the full modular law and specify its square-root branch.

22.4 The heat equation

The derivative convergence proved above permits differentiating every summand twice in zzz or once in τ\tauτ. Their multipliers are −4π2n2-4\pi^2n^2−4π2n2 and πin2\pi\ii n^2πin2, respectively. For a concrete second description, let ft(y)=(4πt)−1/2e−y2/(4t)f_t(y)=(4\pi t)^{-1/2}e^{-y^2/(4t)}ft​(y)=(4πt)−1/2e−y2/(4t). Thus the periodic heat solution is also a sum of translated real Gaussian kernels. Both descriptions follow from convergent series; no initial Dirac distribution is needed for this calculation.

The two heat-solution series organise the same value by different indices. In the Fourier series the higher frequencies are suppressed as time grows. In the Gaussian series the nearby translates describe the small-time concentration directly. Choosing a representation means checking which of these already convergent tails is easier to control, while retaining the same mass and period.

Summary

Periodisation and Fourier uniqueness prove Poisson summation for every Schwartz function. The theta series then has one simple zero per period cell, satisfies the heat equation and obeys the full modular law.

  • The Poisson factor is 2π\sqrt{2\pi}2π​ and the sampled wave numbers are 2πm2\pi m2πm in the course transform convention.
  • Theta is quasi-periodic, with zeros at (1+τ)/2+Z+τZ(1+\tau)/2+\Z+\tau\Z(1+τ)/2+Z+τZ.
  • The root in the modular law is principal and positive for τ/i>0\tau/\ii>0τ/i>0.
