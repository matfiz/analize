Lecture 9

Complex derivatives and Cauchy’s theorem

Complex differentiability requires the same derivative in every direction. We prove that a holomorphic function on a star-shaped domain has a primitive. Its integral around every closed curve in that domain is zero. This result supplies the contour arguments used in the next lectures.

By the end of this lecture you will be able to:

  • Test complex differentiability using the real differential.
  • Differentiate a power series and the principal logarithm on their domains.
  • Prove Goursat’s theorem and construct a primitive on a star-shaped domain.
  • Check when a contour argument can use that construction.

9.4 Goursat’s theorem

For a filled triangle TTT, the symbol ∂T\partial T∂T denotes its boundary traversed counter-clockwise. The whole filled triangle must lie in the open set Ω\OmegaΩ.

Joining the side midpoints divides the triangle into four smaller triangles.

The four small triangles carry the same counter-clockwise orientation. Each shared edge contributes twice with opposite signs.

9.5 A primitive on a star-shaped domain

A domain is a nonempty connected open subset of C\CC. A domain Ω\OmegaΩ is star-shaped about z0∈Ωz_0\in\Omegaz0​∈Ω if the whole segment [z0,z][z_0,z][z0​,z] lies in Ω\OmegaΩ for every z∈Ωz\in\Omegaz∈Ω. Every disk is star-shaped about its centre.

The primitive construction compares the segments from z0z_0z0​ to two nearby points. The disk at zzz supplies the short third side of the triangle.

The shaded triangle lies in the domain because every segment from z0z_0z0​ to a point of the short side lies in the domain.

9.6 Domains used in later contour arguments

The next contour arguments need the following domain checks. A set is convex if it contains the segment joining any two of its points. An open half-disk, for example {z:∣z∣<R, Im⁡z>0}\{z:\abs z<R,\ \Im z>0\}{z:∣z∣<R, Imz>0}, is convex as the intersection of a disk and a half-plane. It is therefore star-shaped about each of its points. The theorem applies to closed curves inside this open half-disk. Boundary contours need a further argument, given in Lecture 11 where they are used to evaluate real integrals.

The keyhole domain V=C∖[0,∞)V=\C\setminus[0,\infty)V=C∖[0,∞) is star-shaped about −1-1−1. Indeed, a point of [−1,z][-1,z][−1,z] has the form −(1−t)+tz-(1-t)+tz−(1−t)+tz, where 0≤t≤10\le t\le10≤t≤1. Lecture 11 uses this slit plane for integrals with a branch cut along the positive ray.

Cauchy’s theorem applies separately on each sector. It does not assert that a holomorphic function on the entire annulus has zero integral around its central hole.

Summary

  • A complex derivative is a real differential given by multiplication by one complex number.
  • Power series are holomorphic. The principal logarithm is holomorphic on its specified slit plane and has derivative 1/z1/z1/z there.
  • Goursat’s theorem gives zero boundary integrals for filled triangles contained in an open set on which the function is holomorphic.
  • On a star-shaped domain, every holomorphic function has a primitive and every closed-curve integral is zero.
