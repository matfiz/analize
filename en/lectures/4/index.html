Lecture 4

Integrals with parameters and in several variables

What this lecture does

An iterated integral needs a hypothesis that permits the change of order. An integral depending on a parameter needs a bound before a limit or a derivative passes inside. We apply these conditions to the Gaussian and then treat an improper integral that is not absolutely integrable.

4.1 Changing the order of integration

The dominated convergence theorem of Theorem 3.5 permits a limit under an integral when a single integrable function bounds the sequence. For two integrations we use the following standing fact. Its cited status has the same inheritance exemption as M1–M3.

Summary

  • Tonelli permits nonnegative integrands. Fubini permits integrable complex integrands. Both include almost-everywhere slice qualifications.
  • A coordinate substitution uses the absolute Jacobian determinant. Polar coordinates give the Gaussian integral.
  • A local integrable majorant justifies continuity. With integrability at one parameter, an integrable majorant on compact subintervals for the parameter derivative justifies differentiation.
  • The Gaussian transform follows from its differential equation.
  • The Dirichlet integral is conditional. Its uniform tail estimate is needed when its regularisation is removed.
