Lecture 13

The Gamma function and Laplace’s method

What this lecture does

Euler’s integral defines the Gamma function in a half-plane. Its functional equation extends the function meromorphically to the plane. We use the integral to evaluate radial integrals and to prove real Stirling asymptotics. A cited sectorial estimate then controls the logarithmic derivative.

Summary

  • Euler’s Gamma integral is holomorphic for Re⁡s>0\Re s>0Res>0. Its recurrence continues it with residues (−1)n/n!(-1)^n/n!(−1)n/n! at −n-n−n.
  • Beta is a quotient of Gamma functions. Reflection follows from the keyhole integral and the identity theorem. Gamma has no zeros.
  • Convergent radial integrals give the stated Gamma quotient. Its continuation defines the dimensional-regularisation prescription.
  • At a unique nondegenerate interior minimum, with a positive outer gap and integrable weighted amplitude, Laplace’s method gives the Gaussian leading term.
  • Real Stirling is proved. Sectorial Stirling is cited, and its holomorphic remainder gives the digamma estimate by Cauchy’s bound.
