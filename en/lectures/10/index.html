Lecture 10

Cauchy’s formula and rigidity

What this lecture does

Cauchy’s formula recovers a holomorphic function inside a disk from its values on the boundary. We derive its power series and the estimates on its derivatives. These results also justify limits and integrals that define holomorphic functions.

10.1 Cauchy’s integral formula on a disk

Recall that D(a,r)={z:∣z−a∣<r}D(a,r)=\{z:\abs{z-a}<r\}D(a,r)={z:∣z−a∣<r}. Its boundary is traversed counter-clockwise. The closed disk must lie inside the open set of holomorphy. An open disk alone does not contain its boundary.

Lecture 9 constructed a single primitive on a star-shaped domain. A region with a hole need not have such a primitive. Instead we use a primitive on each small disk and add the resulting boundary integrals. This proves a statement about the sum of all boundary components.

We first extend the local result from Lecture 9 to the boundaries used here. A region in the following lemma may have holes. Positive orientation keeps the region on the left, so a hole is traversed clockwise. Here a region means an open set lying locally on exactly one side of each smooth boundary arc.

Lemma 10.1 (Boundary cancellation). Let EEE be a bounded region whose boundary consists of finitely many simple closed curves made from line segments and circular arcs. The curves are disjoint. If fff is holomorphic on an open neighbourhood of E‾\overline EE, then ∫∂Ef(ζ) dζ=0.\int_{\partial E}f(\zeta)\,\dd\zeta=0.∫∂E​f(ζ)dζ=0. The integral denotes the sum over all oriented boundary components.

Proof. By Theorem 9.4, a holomorphic function has a primitive on every disk contained in its open set of holomorphy. Cover the compact set E‾\overline EE by smaller disks whose concentric larger disks still lie in that open set. A sufficiently fine square grid has the property that each square meeting E‾\overline EE lies in one larger disk. To see this, choose a finite subcover by the smaller disks and make each square’s diameter smaller than the smallest gap to the corresponding larger circle.

Choose a translated grid with no boundary vertex on a grid line and no grid line tangent to a boundary circle or coincident with a boundary segment. Only finitely many squares meet E‾\overline EE. Each boundary arc meets their grid lines finitely many times. Cutting EEE along the grid therefore gives finitely many pieces inside disks with primitives. Orient each boundary edge with its piece on the left. At each vertex, every local sector belonging to the piece has one entering and one leaving edge. The numbers of incoming and outgoing edges therefore agree. These finitely many directed edges decompose into closed walks: follow unused edges until a vertex repeats, remove the resulting closed walk, and repeat. Removing a closed walk preserves the balance at every vertex, so the process accounts for every edge.

Each walk lies in one disk. Its integral is zero by Equation (9.2), which evaluates a primitive at the endpoints. Add these equalities over all pieces. On an original boundary arc the piece lies on the same side as EEE, so that arc inherits EEE’s orientation. Every internal grid edge separates two pieces on opposite sides and occurs twice with opposite directions. Only the oriented arcs of ∂E\partial E∂E remain. 

The grid in the figure separates the global boundary from the small pieces on which a primitive is available.

The lemma uses local primitives and cancellation. It makes no assertion about the integral around a hole alone. For example, on an annulus the integrals of 1/ζ1/\zeta1/ζ around its outer and inner boundaries cancel.

10.3 Liouville and the fundamental theorem of algebra

An entire function is holomorphic on all of C\CC.

Theorem 10.4 (Liouville). Every bounded entire function is constant.

Proof. If ∣f∣≤M\abs{f}\le M∣f∣≤M on C\CC, Cauchy’s estimate gives ∣f′(a)∣≤M/r\abs{f'(a)}\le M/r∣f′(a)∣≤M/r for every r>0r>0r>0. Letting r→∞r\to\inftyr→∞ shows f′(a)=0f'(a)=0f′(a)=0 at every a∈Ca\in\Ca∈C. Restricting fff to a segment and applying the real fundamental theorem of calculus shows that its endpoint values agree. Thus fff is constant. 

Theorem 10.5 (Fundamental theorem of algebra). Every complex polynomial of degree n≥1n\ge1n≥1 has exactly nnn roots, counted with multiplicity.

Proof. Let p(z)=bnzn+⋯+b0p(z)=b_nz^n+\cdots+b_0p(z)=bn​zn+⋯+b0​ with bn≠0b_n\ne0bn​=0. As ∣z∣→∞\abs z\to\infty∣z∣→∞, the quotient p(z)/znp(z)/z^np(z)/zn tends to bnb_nbn​. For sufficiently large ∣z∣\abs z∣z∣, this gives ∣p(z)∣≥∣bn∣∣z∣n/2\abs{p(z)}\ge\abs{b_n}\abs z^n/2∣p(z)∣≥∣bn​∣∣z∣n/2. If ppp had no root, its continuous modulus would also have a positive minimum on the remaining compact disk. Consequently 1/p1/p1/p would be bounded and entire. Liouville would make 1/p1/p1/p, and hence ppp, constant, a contradiction.

A root aaa gives a factor z−az-az−a by polynomial division. Repeating the existence argument on the quotient gives nnn linear factors, with a nonzero constant left over. No further root is possible in that product. The number of repeated factors at a root is its multiplicity. 

10.4 Identity and maximum modulus

The first nonzero coefficient determines a zero locally. If f(a)=0f(a)=0f(a)=0 and its Taylor series is not identically zero, let m≥1m\ge1m≥1 be the first index with nonzero coefficient. Factoring the series gives f(z)=(z−a)mg(z),g(a)≠0,f(z)=(z-a)^m g(z),\qquad g(a)\ne0,f(z)=(z−a)mg(z),g(a)=0, where ggg is holomorphic near aaa. By continuity, ggg stays nonzero on a smaller disk. The zero aaa is therefore isolated and has order mmm.

Theorem 10.6 (Identity theorem). Let fff and ggg be holomorphic on a domain Ω\OmegaΩ. If they agree on a sequence of distinct points converging to a point of Ω\OmegaΩ, then f=gf=gf=g throughout Ω\OmegaΩ.

Proof. Apply the preceding factorisation to f−gf-gf−g at the limit point. Isolated zeros cannot accumulate there, so all Taylor coefficients vanish and f−gf-gf−g vanishes on a disk. Let UUU be the set of points where f−gf-gf−g vanishes on a neighbourhood. This set is nonempty and open. It is also closed relative to Ω\OmegaΩ: a point in its relative closure either belongs to UUU already or is a limit of distinct zeros, to which the same Taylor argument applies. Connectedness means that Ω\OmegaΩ cannot be split into disjoint nonempty relatively open sets. If UUU were a proper subset, it and its complement would give such a split, since UUU is both open and relatively closed. Thus U=ΩU=\OmegaU=Ω. 

An analytic continuation of fff to a larger domain is a holomorphic function there agreeing with fff on its original domain. The theorem proves uniqueness on a fixed larger domain. It does not assert existence.

Theorem 10.7 (Maximum modulus principle). If a holomorphic function on a domain has a local maximum of its modulus at an interior point, it is constant.

Proof. Suppose the maximum occurs at aaa. If f(a)=0f(a)=0f(a)=0, the local bound forces f=0f=0f=0 on a disk, and the identity theorem finishes the proof. Otherwise multiply fff by a constant of modulus one so that its value at aaa is the positive number M=∣f(a)∣M=\abs{f(a)}M=∣f(a)∣. On each sufficiently small circle about aaa, Cauchy’s formula at the centre gives M=12π∫02πf(a+reit) dt.M=\frac1{2\pi}\int_0^{2\pi}f(a+re^{\ii t})\,\dd t.M=2π1​∫02π​f(a+reit)dt. On this circle, Re⁡f≤∣f∣≤M\Re f\le\abs f\le MRef≤∣f∣≤M. The continuous nonnegative function M−Re⁡fM-\Re fM−Ref has zero integral, so it is zero everywhere on the circle. The modulus bound then forces the imaginary part to vanish. Thus f=Mf=Mf=M on every such circle and on the enclosed disk. The identity theorem extends the equality to the domain. 

In particular, if fff is holomorphic on a neighbourhood of a closed disk, its maximum modulus on the disk is attained on the boundary. Otherwise an interior maximiser would make it constant, in which case the boundary also attains the maximum.

Summary

  • Cauchy’s formula requires a closed disk inside an open holomorphic set.
  • A holomorphic function is analytic. Cauchy’s estimates bound all derivatives by boundary values.
  • Bounded entire functions are constant. Every nonconstant complex polynomial factors into linear factors.
  • On a domain, accumulating interior agreement gives identity, and an interior local maximum of the modulus forces constancy.
  • Compact-set integrable domination makes holomorphic parameter integrals holomorphic and permits all complex derivatives under the integral.
