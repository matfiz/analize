Lecture 17

Explicit meromorphic expansions and products

The poles of the cotangent and the zeros of the sine determine explicit series and product formulas. We prove their convergence and normalisation. A finite Beta integral then gives the reciprocal-Gamma product without using a general factorisation theorem.

By the end of this lecture you will be able to:

  • Justify local uniform convergence of the products used here.
  • Derive the cotangent expansion by a contour estimate.
  • Recover the sine product from its logarithmic derivative.
  • Derive the reciprocal-Gamma product from finite Beta integrals.

17.1 Convergence of holomorphic products

An infinite product ∏n≥1(1+un(z))\prod_{n\ge1}(1+u_n(z))∏n≥1​(1+un​(z)) means the limit of its finite products, in the stated order. A limit of nonzero numbers can be zero, so convergence alone does not describe its zeros. The following criterion also controls the tail away from zero.

Lemma 17.1. Let unu_nun​ be holomorphic on an open set UUU. Locally its zeros, with multiplicity, are exactly the zeros of its factors, provided no factor vanishes identically on the component.

The limit is holomorphic by the locally uniform limit result in §10.6.

On a compact disk inside UUU, all sufficiently late terms satisfy ∣un∣≤1/2|u_n|\le1/2∣un​∣≤1/2. 17.2 Partial fractions for the cotangent

The function πcot⁡(πz)\pi\cot(\pi z)πcot(πz) has a simple pole of residue one at each integer. A sum of individual fractions 1/(z−n)1/(z-n)1/(z−n) would require a summation convention. Pairing positive and negative integers gives convergent terms.

17.3 The sine product

17.5 General existence theorems

A subset of C\CC is discrete here if it has no finite accumulation point. An entire function other than zero has a discrete zero set by the identity theorem. The converse is an existence theorem.

Theorem 17.5 (Weierstrass cited). For any discrete set of points in C\CC and any assigned positive integer multiplicity at each point, there is an entire function whose zeros are exactly those points with those multiplicities.

Theorem 17.6 (Mittag–Leffler cited). At each point aaa of a discrete subset of C\CC, prescribe a finite principal part ∑j=1maca,j(z−a)−j\sum_{j=1}^{m_a}c_{a,j}(z-a)^{-j}∑j=1ma​​ca,j​(z−a)−j. There is a meromorphic function with exactly those principal parts and no other poles.

For Weierstrass existence see Stein–Shakarchi, Complex Analysis, Chapter 5, Theorem 4.1, p. 145. Mittag–Leffler existence is Freitag–Busam, Complex Analysis, Theorem IV.3.1. The proofs of these existence statements are external. They require convergence corrections to arbitrary zero or pole data. The explicit cotangent, sine and reciprocal-Gamma proofs above supplied their own corrections and normalisations. They do not depend on either cited theorem. In particular, specifying zeros alone does not determine an entire function uniquely: multiplication by eh(z)e^{h(z)}eh(z) for entire hhh preserves all its zeros and their orders.

Summary

  • Locally summable factor errors give locally uniform holomorphic products. Their sufficiently late tails are nonzero.
  • Pairing the integer poles gives the proved cotangent expansion. That expansion is the logarithmic derivative of the normalised sine product.
  • Finite Beta integrals and the Euler constant give the proved reciprocal-Gamma product with factors (1+s/n)e−s/n(1+s/n)e^{-s/n}(1+s/n)e−s/n.
  • General zero and principal-part existence are cited separately.

Their reciprocal-Gamma proof uses general factorisation; the Beta-limit proof here is independent of it. E. Freitag and R.
