Lecture 26

Hardy space and the Khalfin obstruction

What this lecture does

Positive-time square-integrable data give holomorphy in the upper half-plane. A different support condition, a lower bound on a spectral variable, obstructs exponential decay of a scalar Fourier integral. We prove this obstruction for a nonzero nonnegative integrable density. Applying it to a Hamiltonian requires a cited spectral representation and an explicit absolute-continuity assumption.

26.2 The cited converse

Support and half-plane analyticity must be paired with a growth or norm condition. Holomorphy alone does not imply positive-time support. For example, a nonzero constant is holomorphic on H\HHH but its boundary function is not square-integrable, so it does not belong to this theorem.

The lower support bound and the strip have different variables.

Exponential decay in time would extend the spectral density to this open strip. The density labels refer to the real axis; the almost-everywhere equality holds on the whole real axis. Its zero interval would then force the whole extension to vanish.

26.5 The conditional quantum interpretation

For a self-adjoint Hamiltonian HHH and a normalised vector ψ\psiψ, the spectral theorem gives a probability measure μψ\mu_\psiμψ​ such that ⟨ψ,e−iHt/ℏψ⟩=∫Re−iλt/ℏ dμψ(λ).\inner\psi{e^{-\ii Ht/\hbar}\psi} =\int_\R e^{-\ii\lambda t/\hbar}\,\dd\mu_\psi(\lambda).⟨ψ,e−iHt/ℏψ⟩=∫R​e−iλt/ℏdμψ​(λ). This Hamiltonian-to-measure statement is cited from Teschl, Ch. 3. For unbounded Hamiltonians its functional calculus is outside this course. If HHH is bounded below by E0E_0E0​, the measure is supported in [E0,∞)[E_0,\infty)[E0​,∞). It need not have a density. An eigenvector, for example, gives a point mass.

Corollary 26.4 (Quantum consequence sketched). Suppose the cited spectral representation applies and dμψ(λ)=ρ(λ) dλ\dd\mu_\psi(\lambda)=\rho(\lambda)\,\dd\lambdadμψ​(λ)=ρ(λ)dλ with a nonnegative L1L^1L1 density supported in a half-line. Then the survival amplitude has no eventual exponential upper bound with positive rate.

The scalar proof is complete. The tag records the cited spectral bridge. The assertion here does not extend that proof to arbitrary singular measures. A resonance approximation may describe an intermediate time interval without giving the forbidden bound for every sufficiently large time.

Summary

  • Positive-time L2L^2L2 data give an upper-half-plane Hardy function isometrically. The onto Paley–Wiener converse is cited.
  • Integrable Fourier data with integrable transform satisfy inversion almost everywhere.
  • A nonzero nonnegative L1L^1L1 spectral density supported in a half-line cannot have an eventually exponentially bounded amplitude.
  • The quantum corollary requires both the cited spectral bridge and an absolutely continuous scalar spectral measure.
