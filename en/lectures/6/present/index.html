Lecture 6

Fourier series

An integrable periodic function has Fourier coefficients whether or not its Fourier series converges pointwise. We prove that averaging the partial sums recovers every continuous periodic function uniformly. This gives the Fourier basis of L2(T)L^2(\T)L2(T) and Parseval’s identity. Pointwise convergence requires a separate argument and separate hypotheses.

By the end of this lecture you will be able to:

  • Prove uniform convergence of the Fejér means for continuous periodic functions.
  • Expand an L2L^2L2 function in the Fourier basis and apply Parseval’s identity.
  • Relate periodic smoothness to coefficient decay and uniform convergence.
  • Prove completeness of the sine basis on a finite interval.

6.3 Completeness and Parseval

A trigonometric polynomial is a finite linear combination of the functions ene_nen​. Each Fejér mean is such a polynomial, so Fejér’s theorem proves their uniform density in C(T)C(\T)C(T).

They are also dense in L2(T)L^2(\T)L2(T). To see this, M6 (Theorem 5.4) approximates any L2(−π,π)L^2(-\pi,\pi)L2(−π,π) function by a continuous function compactly supported in (−π,π)(-\pi,\pi)(−π,π). Summary

  • Fejér means recover every continuous periodic function uniformly. Their density proves the Fourier basis theorem and Parseval in L2(T)L^2(\T)L2(T).
  • Periodic C1C^1C1 smoothness gives absolute and uniform convergence of the ordinary Fourier series.
  • The separate Dirichlet statement gives the mean of a jump under piecewise C1C^1C1 hypotheses.
  • Odd extension gives the complete sine basis on (0,L)(0,L)(0,L), for L>0L>0L>0.
