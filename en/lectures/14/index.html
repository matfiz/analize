Lecture 14

Asymptotics of integrals

What this lecture does

A large parameter can concentrate an integral near an endpoint or a stationary point of its phase. We prove Watson’s lemma for a decaying exponential and compute the Fresnel integral by contour rotation. Stationary phase then gives oscillatory leading terms. The Airy integral shows how contour deformation turns an oscillatory integral into a Gaussian approximation.

Summary

  • An asymptotic expansion controls every fixed finite truncation.
  • For β>−1\beta>-1β>−1, Watson’s lemma uses smoothness near zero and an exponential growth bound to extract the endpoint coefficients.
  • With the stated smoothness and support assumptions, a nondegenerate stationary point with nonzero amplitude gives order x−1/2x^{-1/2}x−1/2. A double-zero amplitude gives order x−3/2x^{-3/2}x−3/2 when endpoint terms are absent.
  • The Airy contour gives its differential equation and its exponentially small positive-argument asymptotic. The libration period diverges logarithmically as the modulus tends to one from below.
