Lecture 2

Ordinary differential equations: the mechanics contract

What this lecture does

A differential equation determines a motion from its initial state when its right-hand side has sufficient regularity. We construct the motion by contraction. We then identify its maximal time interval and prove that a finite endpoint forces escape from every compact subset of the state space. These solutions define the flow used in mechanics.

2.6 Global existence and the pendulum

A solution is global if its interval is all of R\RR. A field is complete if every maximal solution is global. Here completeness concerns time of existence, rather than completeness of a normed space. A growth bound gives one sufficient condition.

Summary

  • A C1C^1C1 autonomous field on an open set has a unique maximal solution through each state. At a finite endpoint it eventually leaves every compact subset of that open set.
  • A Lipschitz constant LLL on a common region gives the separation bound eL∣t∣∥z−w∥e^{L\abs t}\norm{z-w}eL∣t∣∥z−w∥ while both paths remain there.
  • The flow has an open domain and satisfies the group law whenever the indicated times belong to its maximal intervals. Smooth dependence and the variational equation were sketched.
  • At most linear growth on all of Rm\R^mRm ensures global existence. The pendulum is global by its energy bound.
