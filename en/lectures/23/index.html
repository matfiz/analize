Lecture 23

Analytic differential equations

What this lecture does

Complex Picard iteration gives analytic solutions at ordinary points. At a regular singular point, powers replace Taylor series. We prove the nonresonant Frobenius construction and use the Gauss equation to connect series, integrals and continuation around a singular point.

23.4 Continuation around a singular point

Recall from §19.1 that a germ records a holomorphic function near a point. Two such functions represent the same germ if they agree on some neighbourhood of that point. Continuation of a local solution around a loop can change its germ. This change is its monodromy along that loop. The Frobenius factors above change by e2πirje^{2\pi\ii r_j}e2πirj​ around zero, while their analytic coefficient functions return to their original values. The loops and the chosen cuts have different roles in this statement.

The cuts select single branches. A continuation loop crosses a cut and tracks the analytic germ rather than reassigning the principal value.

For a=b=1a=b=1a=b=1 and c=2c=2c=2, the exponents at zero are 000 and −1-1−1. At one both exponents are zero. This example is resonant at both points. 23.5 Two other equation families

The Kummer equation is zy′′+(c−z)y′−ay=0zy''+(c-z)y'-ay=0zy′′+(c−z)y′−ay=0. The Bessel equation of order μ\muμ is z2y′′+zy′+(z2−μ2)y=0z^2y''+zy'+(z^2-\mu^2)y=0z2y′′+zy′+(z2−μ2)y=0. The order μ\muμ is distinct from the frequency ν\nuν of the Fourier convention. Both equations have at most a regular singular point at zero. Their indicial polynomials are respectively r(r+c−1)r(r+c-1)r(r+c−1) and r2−μ2r^2-\mu^2r2−μ2. Thus the Frobenius mechanism applies when the root difference is nonintegral. Their global connection formulas are not treated here. In quantum radial equations the choice of a solution at one endpoint does not by itself determine its behaviour at the other.
