Lecture 25

The Newton kernel and outgoing waves

An exponentially damped radial kernel gives fundamental solutions in three dimensions. Its Fourier transform proves the weak equation. Removing the damping gives the Newton kernel or an outgoing Helmholtz wave, with the side of the distributional boundary value fixed by the damping sign.

By the end of this lecture you will be able to:

  • Compute the Fourier transform of the damped radial kernel.
  • Prove its fundamental-solution identity using weak derivatives.
  • Derive the outgoing multiplier and its delta contribution on a sphere.
  • Solve a compactly supported smooth forcing equation by convolution.

25.6 Comparison with evolution kernels

An initial-data kernel propagates data prescribed at time zero. It solves a different problem from a stationary spatial fundamental solution. The following comparison is cited from Strichartz, Ch. 5. The equations in this comparison have dimensionless coefficients.
