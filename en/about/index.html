About this site

The companion to Real and Complex Analysis — parts i and ii.

What is here

28 lectures with 3 definitions, 148 numbered results, 132 proofs, 1 worked example and 16 figures, and 0 problems on 0 exercise sheets so far. Everything is generated from the same LaTeX source as the printed notes: a theorem here is the same object, with the same number, as the theorem in the PDF, and every number on the site is checked against the typeset volume before the site is built.

Part I has fourteen lectures. Its first block develops limits, integration, differential equations and Fourier analysis. Its second block develops complex differentiation, contour integration, special functions and asymptotics. Part II has fourteen further lectures, from conformal maps and elliptic functions to differential equations and boundary-value problems.

Reading independently

The lectures divide a self-reading course into convenient units. There is no prescribed reading time. The course assumes calculus in one and several variables, series, complex arithmetic, linear differential equations and basic normed/Hilbert-space vocabulary from linear algebra. Measure theory, distributions, spectral theory and quantum mechanics are developed or explicitly supplied here rather than assumed.

The Gaussian connects integration, Fourier analysis, asymptotics and theta functions. The pendulum connects existence, its period and elliptic functions. Cauchy integrals connect boundary values and continuation. Part I does not depend on the later uses in Part II.

How much is proved

The notes are honest about rigour. A result with no tag is proved in full. A result tagged sketched is given with its central idea; one tagged cited is used, and its proof lives in the references. A deduction using a cited tool keeps the appropriate qualification. The declared exception is the standing measure-theoretic facts M1–M6 in Lectures 3–5: a result proved from them counts as proved. Their hypotheses must still be checked.

Questions and solutions

Each lecture has short self-check questions with hints and feedback. Attempt an answer before opening a hint. Then reconstruct a worked example and try a fresh case. These tasks support independent use; a score does not establish every proof-oriented outcome.

Outcome ticks are your own assessment. Your first-attempt tally and the review schedule record different evidence: recovery after help is distinguished from unaided retrieval. Separate exercise sheets have not been published. Their full worked solutions remain private.

Your privacy

There is no account, no login, and nothing is sent anywhere. Your theme, study progress and annotations are stored in your own browser.

Found a mistake?

When reporting a mistake, give the lecture number, the statement or question and the step that needs correction. Corrections are made in the source so the notes and website can be updated together.

Colophon

Built as a static site from LaTeX. Pandoc preserves the notes' own definition, theorem, example and remark structure; the printed numbering and every cross-reference are recovered from the source and checked against the typeset volume; KaTeX renders the mathematics at build time with the course's own notation macros, translated automatically from the style file the PDFs load; the diagrams are the same TikZ drawings as in the PDF and the data figures the same generated plots, converted to SVG. The notes are set in Charter, the face the printed notes use. No tracking, no cookies, no third-party requests.
