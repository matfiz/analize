About this site

The companion to Real and Complex Analysis — parts i and ii.

What is here

28 lectures with 3 definitions, 148 numbered results, 132 proofs, 0 worked examples and 6 figures, and 0 problems on 0 exercise sheets so far. Everything is generated from the same LaTeX source as the printed notes: a theorem here is the same object, with the same number, as the theorem in the PDF, and every number on the site is checked against the typeset volume before the site is built.

Part I has fourteen lectures. Its first block develops limits, integration, differential equations and Fourier analysis. Its second block develops complex differentiation, contour integration, special functions and asymptotics. Part II has fourteen further lectures, from conformal maps and elliptic functions to differential equations and boundary-value problems.

How much is proved

The notes are honest about rigour. A result with no tag is proved in full. A result tagged sketched is given with its central idea; one tagged cited is used, and its proof lives in the references. The standing measure-theoretic facts are listed in the course's proof policy. Each application must still check their hypotheses.

Questions and solutions

An exercise is a question, and a solution is the full worked method. This site is always the questions-only edition: worked solutions are done in the tutorial, which is where the learning in them actually happens.

They are not merely hidden here. Every solution is deleted from the source text before it reaches the converter, the public sheet PDFs are recompiled as the questions-only variant rather than copied from the repository, and the finished site and every PDF in it are searched for solution material before anything can be published.

Your privacy

There is no account, no login, and nothing is sent anywhere. Your theme, study progress and annotations are stored in your own browser.

Found a mistake?

Please report it. Corrections during a course's first run are useful — bring it to a lecture or tutorial and it will be fixed at source and republished.

Colophon

Built as a static site from LaTeX. Pandoc preserves the notes' own definition, theorem, example and remark structure; the printed numbering and every cross-reference are recovered from the source and checked against the typeset volume; KaTeX renders the mathematics at build time with the course's own notation macros, translated automatically from the style file the PDFs load; the diagrams are the same TikZ drawings as in the PDF and the data figures the same generated plots, converted to SVG. The notes are set in Charter, the face the printed notes use. No tracking, no cookies, no third-party requests.
